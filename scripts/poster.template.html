Threat timing is less stable.

Across the same 14 dates, mean Victory scores rise from 0.694 before to 0.844 on the event day, then fall to 0.729 after. These are descriptive model outputs; frame accuracy has not been established.

01 Question & workflow

Do rhetorical frames differ around conflict dates? Political posts can mix threats, success claims, and negotiations. Comparing those frames may help readers examine how public messaging changes around selected events.

Task. Given an Iran-related post, assign six independent frame scores, then compare the day before, day of, and day after an event’s recorded start date.

  1. Collect & alignPrepared public-post CSV + UCDP-derived event workbook. Match Eastern Time calendar dates to event start dates at −1, 0, +1 days.
  2. Prepare the textUse saved content_text; deduplicate by post ID. Preserve case and punctuation. Keep one post / event-date pair.
  3. Represent & classifyBART tokenizer → contextual representations → facebook/bart-large-mnli. Six natural-language frame descriptions; multi-label NLI, truncation enabled.
  4. Aggregate & compareAverage post scores within each event-date/window, then average those date means. Primary comparison uses the same 14 dates in every window.

The classifier sees post text only. Event headlines, fatalities, and severity do not enter the model. A post may receive several high scores.

Limits & responsible use

Association only. These selected windows cannot establish causation or predict military action. Same-day dates do not prove that a post preceded an event within that day.

Overlapping windows reuse posts and create dependence. Missing posts are not zero rhetoric. Linked headlines and media-only posts can distort attribution; truncated text can lose context.

Practical implication. Use the scores to locate language worth inspecting alongside its source. Human validation and a non-event comparison are needed before stronger claims.

Inspect model behavior

These two cases illustrate questions for annotation. They do not estimate an error rate and were selected after inspecting scores.

How were the means calculated?

For each frame and event date, average the scores of the unique posts in that date/window. Then take the unweighted mean of those event-date means. A busy event date gets the same weight as a quiet date. Missing groups are omitted, never replaced by zero. The primary view retains only dates with nonempty groups at all three offsets.

The secondary view reproduces the original notebook’s available-date estimator. Neither view has a non-event control group or a causal design. We do not treat overlapping event dates as independent observations or report significance tests.

What exactly does the model receive?

The original notebook sends saved content_text to the Transformers zero-shot pipeline with multi_label=True, truncation=True, and hypothesis template This text expresses {}. It scores each unique post once. The prepared text is not stemmed, stopword-filtered, or lowercased by an additional preprocessing step in that notebook. No training or fine-tuning is performed here.

  • Threat: military threats, escalation, retaliation, ultimatums, warnings, strikes, or destruction directed at Iran.
  • Victory: claims of victory, military success, Iranian weakness, defeat, surrender, decimation, or successful U.S. operations.
  • Diplomacy: diplomacy, negotiations, talks, deals, conditions, agreements, or communication with Iran or allied governments.
  • Ceasefire / Ending: a ceasefire, stopping military operations, ending the conflict, pausing attacks, or declaring the fighting essentially finished.
  • Blame Allies: blaming or criticizing NATO, European allies, or other countries for not helping enough or being unhelpful.
  • Media Criticism: criticizing, blaming, or attacking the mainstream news media, journalists, or press coverage as biased, fake, or dishonest.

Questions to rehearse

Why not simply average every post?

Some dates contain more posts. Averaging within event-date/window first prevents those dates from receiving extra weight just because Trump posted more. Equal date weights still do not solve selection bias or overlapping windows.

Why can multiple frames score highly?

The model runs in multi-label mode, evaluating each frame separately. These six scores do not sum to one. A post can combine an ultimatum and negotiation language.

What does 0.844 mean?

It is the mean Victory entailment score after the two aggregation steps, on the same 14 dates. It does not mean that 84.4% of posts express victory or that the classification is 84.4% accurate.

Where could leakage or invalid inference occur?

Event metadata is excluded from model input. But selected windows and nearby dates reuse posts; splitting repeated rows could put the same post in development and test. Evaluation must split by unique post ID. A predictive design would additionally require strict temporal cutoffs and non-event dates.
