01 Frame the project

How do rhetorical frames differ around conflict dates? Political posts mix threats, success claims, and negotiations. Comparing frames helps readers examine public messaging around selected events.

Task. Given an Iran-related post, compare six independent frame scores before, on, and after selected event starts; contrast opening-period and later starts.

02 Explain the workflow

  1. Collect & align__EVENT_COUNT__ selected UCDP records; __DATE_COUNT__ start dates. Match original Iran-related posts by Eastern Time dates at −1, 0, +1 days.
  2. PrepareDeduplicate by post ID; preserve saved text, case, and punctuation. One pair per post / event date.
  3. Represent & classifyBART tokenizer → contextual representations → facebook/bart-large-mnli. Six natural-language prompts; multi-label NLI; truncation enabled.
  4. AggregateAverage within each date/window, then across date means. Compare opening-period starts (Feb 28–Mar 13) with later starts.

The model sees post text only. Event metadata and fatality totals do not enter the classifier. One post can score highly on several frames.

04 Evaluate & reflect

Preliminary · human evaluation pending

Integrity checks passed. All __POST_COUNT__ score IDs join to the corpus; six score columns are finite and in 0–1. Pairs are unique; saved date offsets match.

Accuracy is untested. No human gold labels, held-out precision/recall/F1, or evaluated baseline. Integrity checks do not validate rhetorical accuracy.

A failure mode to investigate

Limits & practical implications

Window coverage differs; missing posts are not zero rhetoric. Overlapping windows reuse posts and create dependence.

Same-day dates cannot establish ordering. Linked headlines, missing media, and truncation can distort attribution.

Use scores to find language for human review. Human validation and non-event comparisons are needed before stronger claims.
