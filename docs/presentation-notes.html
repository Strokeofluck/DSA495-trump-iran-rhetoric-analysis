A 90-second explanation

Question, 15 seconds. “I’m examining how six rhetorical frames vary around selected conflict dates in Trump’s Iran-related posts.”

Method, 25 seconds. “I score each post once with BART-MNLI using six frame descriptions. I average within date and window, then across dates. I include all available posts; each window uses every date with posts in that window.”

Finding, 25 seconds. “Victory scores peak on event days. Exploratorily, deadlier event dates also tend to have higher Victory scores in next-day posts: rho is 0.50 across 20 dates, or 0.43 without the largest date. These are descriptive associations, not predictions.”

Limits, 25 seconds. “These are unvalidated model scores on selected dates. Posts recur across windows. The results do not show prediction or causation. My next priority is human annotation and held-out evaluation.”

Questions to rehearse

Why not simply average every post?

Some dates contain more posts. Averaging within event-date/window first prevents those dates from receiving extra weight just because Trump posted more. Equal date weights still do not solve selection bias or overlapping windows.

Why can multiple frames score highly?

The model runs in multi-label mode, evaluating each frame separately. These six scores do not sum to one. A post can combine an ultimatum and negotiation language.

What does 0.761 mean?

It is the mean Victory entailment score for the event-day window after the two aggregation steps. It does not mean that 76.1% of posts express victory or that the classification is 76.1% accurate.

Where could leakage or invalid inference occur?

Event metadata is excluded from model input. But selected windows and nearby dates reuse posts; splitting repeated rows could put the same post in development and test. Evaluation must split by unique post ID. A predictive design would additionally require strict temporal cutoffs and non-event dates.

What did AI do, and what must I explain?

Codex assisted with coding, documentation, analysis presentation, and this poster. BART produced the saved frame scores. Read the aggregation script and original notebook, verify examples, and be able to explain the comparison population, frame prompts, limitations, and your own decisions. The poster does not establish personal understanding by itself.

What still needs work for the full rubric

The page addresses the required structure and exposes the available evidence. It cannot substitute for missing evaluation or your oral defense. The instructor’s rubric assigns 30% to experimental evidence and 20% to oral command.

  1. Human evaluation: define frame annotation rules; label about 30 unique posts without seeing scores. Keep development and held-out posts separate. Compare BART with a declared simple baseline, report per-frame precision, recall, F1 and support, and inspect errors. This is a plan, not a completed experiment.
  2. Provenance & clean rerun: pin the source snapshot, UCDP release, model revision, and package versions. Resolve long-post truncation and media/linked-headline attribution. The missing six-frame style mappings in the original notebook’s HTML renderer were repaired; a fresh full Colab run remains to be documented.
